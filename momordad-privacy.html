1Who we are

Mom or Dad? (“the app”, listed in some App Stores as “Who Do I Look Like? Mom or Dad”, “Mum or Dad” in the UK and “¿A quién me parezco? Mamá/Papá” in Mexico) is made and published by Fatih Alkan, an independent app developer based in Türkiye (“we”, “us”). This policy explains how the app handles information, and it covers the iPhone app and this page. You can reach us any time at support@alkanfatih.com.

2The short version

We do not collect your personal data. The app has no accounts, no sign-in and no servers of its own. It never sends your photos, your face or your results anywhere. Two connections do happen, both about the subscription: Apple's App Store (prices, the introductory offer, a purchase or a restore) and RevenueCat, the service that manages subscriptions for us. Tapping a link — like the one that opened this page — hands it to Safari.

Everything to do with your photos and faces is processed only on your device, which under Apple's definition is not “collected”. The one thing that is collected is your subscription status, and the App Store privacy label declares it as Purchases — not linked to your identity and never used to track you.

3What happens to your photos and faces

Choosing a photo from your library

When you tap Choose from photos, iOS opens Apple's system photo picker. The picker runs outside the app: the app receives only the single picture you pick and has no access to the rest of your library. That is why the app never asks for permission to read your photo library — it cannot browse, scan or read it at all.

Taking a photo with the camera

When you tap Take photo, the app explains what the camera is for and then iOS asks for camera permission. The camera is active only while that photo is being taken, it opens on the front camera, and no video or audio is ever recorded. The picture is handed to the app in memory; the app does not save it to your photo library or anywhere else.

Comparing the faces

Everything below runs on your iPhone, inside the app's private sandbox, with no internet connection involved:

  • Apple's Vision framework, which is part of iOS, finds the face in each photo and locates landmarks — the eyes, brows, nose, lips and the outline of the face. If a photo contains several faces, the largest one is used.
  • The three faces are rotated and scaled so that the eyes line up in the same place, which is what makes a fair comparison and the split portrait possible.
  • Small crops (eyes, brows, nose, mouth) are compared with Apple's on-device image feature prints; the jaw is compared as a shape; the cheeks are compared as a colour.
  • The app turns those comparisons into the percentages you see, and into the one-line verdict.

The landmarks, the crops, the feature prints and the aligned images exist only in your iPhone's memory while you are using the app. They go when you tap Start a new strip, when you remove a photo (that photo and everything worked out from the strip), when you withdraw your consent in Settings, and when iOS ends the app. Leaving the app on your Home screen keeps them in memory for as long as iOS keeps the app alive, which is what lets you come back to your strip. They are never written to storage, and never sent anywhere.

When the app leaves the foreground, iOS photographs the screen to draw the card you see in the app switcher. The app pulls a plain curtain over itself first, so that picture shows the booth's sign instead of anybody's face.

What the app is not doing

The app does not identify, recognise, verify or authenticate anyone. It holds no database of people, it cannot tell who somebody is, and it cannot recognise the same person in a different photo. It only measures how alike three pictures look, and it is not a DNA, genetic, paternity or medical test of any kind.

“Skin tone” is one of the six things compared. It measures the colour of the cheeks as they appear in the photos you chose — which lighting, the camera and a suntan all change. It is not a measure of ethnicity, race or ancestry, and it is not stored beyond the score it produces.

4What is stored on your iPhone

All of it lives in the app's own sandbox on your device, protected by iOS Data Protection. None of it is ours, none of it is sent to us, and deleting the app deletes all of it.

WhatWhyHow long
Saved results For each strip you make: one 64-bit code per photo, the six feature scores, the overall result and the date. The code is a “difference hash” — the aligned face is turned into a tiny 9×8 grey thumbnail and each of the 64 bits records whether one pixel is brighter than the next one along. It is computed from brightness, not from measurements of the face; it cannot be turned back into a picture or a face; at 64 bits it holds far too little to tell people apart, and it is never used to identify anyone or compared with anything other than the strips you made yourself. It is a matching key, so two nearly identical copies of the same photo produce nearly identical codes — which is exactly its job. It exists so that re-running the same three photos gives you the same result — and doesn't use up a free strip. 5What leaves your iPhone

  • Nothing about your photos leaves the device. We run no servers of our own, and the app has no code of its own that sends anything: not your photos, not your faces, not your results, not a crash report.
  • The subscription goes through RevenueCat. To know whether you are a subscriber, the app uses RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., United States), which receives an anonymous customer id the app generates, the purchase and renewal details Apple reports for that id, and basic technical facts such as your country, device model, iOS version and app version. It never receives your photos, your faces, your results or your name — we don't have those either. We use it only to unlock Pro and to see aggregate subscription numbers, never to identify you or to advertise to you. If you want that record deleted, write to us and we will ask RevenueCat to delete it.
  • What Apple shows us anyway. Like every developer, we can see Apple's own App Store reports: how many people downloaded, updated, subscribed or cancelled, in aggregate, by country and device type — and, if you have turned on “Share With App Developers” in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements, Apple's anonymised crash and usage statistics. Apple prepares all of it; it never names you, and it tells us nothing about your photos or your results.
  • Only what you share, when you share it. If you use the share button, the picture you chose (your photo strip or the split portrait, with the percentages on it) goes to whichever app or person you pick in the iOS share sheet. From that point it is governed by that service's terms and privacy policy, not ours. Shared pictures are newly drawn by the app, so they do not carry the location or camera information that may have been in your original photos.
  • Purchases are handled by Apple. Subscriptions are bought through Apple's in-app purchase system. Apple processes the payment; we never see your name, your email, your address or your payment details. From Apple's StoreKit the app learns the prices and billing periods of the plans, whether your Apple Account is still eligible for the introductory offer, and whether a subscription is currently active — nothing else, and none of it reaches us. See Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • The rating prompt shown by the app is Apple's standard one. Your star rating goes to the App Store and we never learn how you rated. If you go on to write a public review, we see it the way everyone does: Apple publishes it with the nickname you gave Apple and your country, and shows it to us in App Store Connect.
  • If you email us at support@alkanfatih.com, we receive your email address and whatever you write (please don't send photos — we don't need them). Our mailbox is hosted in Türkiye by our email provider, which stores the message for us and does nothing else with it. We use your message only to answer you, never for marketing, and we delete the correspondence once it is no longer needed. You can ask us to delete it sooner.
  • No one else. Apart from Apple and RevenueCat above, we share no personal data with anyone, and neither of them ever receives a photo, a face or a result. The only other recipient is whoever you choose yourself in the share sheet.

6Face data: our commitments and deletion schedule

Some laws treat information derived from a face as especially sensitive, whether or not it can identify anyone. We take that seriously, so these are binding commitments:

  • Face landmarks and the comparisons made from them are used for one purpose only: to work out the resemblance between the three photos you chose, on your device, after you have agreed.
  • They are never used for identification, verification, authentication, security, marketing, advertising, profiling or mining of any other kind — by us or by anyone else. We never sell, lease, trade, disclose, share or otherwise profit from them.
  • They are never transmitted. We never receive them, never possess them and have no technical ability to access, modify or use them. There is no face database anywhere, and no way to match a face across users, photos or devices.
  • The app asks for your explicit agreement before the first photo is added, in writing, on screen, and tells you what is kept and for how long. You can withdraw it at any time in Settings → Withdraw face consent, which also deletes every saved result from your iPhone. If we ever change what the app does with faces, it explains the change and asks again.
  • Only you can give that agreement, and only for what happens on your own iPhone. Because nothing about your mother, your father or anyone else you photograph ever reaches us, we hold, control and can do nothing with their faces either — which is also why you must only add photos of people who are happy for you to use them (see the Terms of Use).

8Children, and photos of other people

The app is a general-audience app and is not directed to children under 13. The agreement to face analysis should be given by an adult: if the person using the app is under 18 — or under the age their country sets for agreeing to this kind of processing — a parent or guardian should set it up and agree on their behalf. It collects no personal information from anyone — adult or child — because nothing ever leaves the device. If you are a parent or guardian using the app with your child, or comparing a photo of your own child with photos of their parents, those photos are analysed only on your iPhone and are never uploaded, never stored by the app and never used to identify anyone. Nothing you do in the app is reported to us, so there is nothing for us to delete; if you want everything gone from the device, use Settings → Clear saved results or delete the app.

The same is true for your mother's and father's photos, and anyone else you add: their pictures are analysed on your phone only, nothing about them is retained except the non-reversible code described above, and they are never identified, catalogued or shared by us. Please add photos of other people only if they are happy for you to do so — and if a child is pictured, only if you are their parent or guardian or have that parent's permission. This is also required by our Terms of Use.

If you are the person in someone else's photo: everything about you stayed on their iPhone. We have no copy, no database and no way to find, change or delete anything — only the person holding the phone can, by clearing the saved results or deleting the app. If you are worried about a picture that was shared with you or about you, ask them, and write to us if you would like help understanding what the app does.

9No tracking, no ads, no analytics

The app contains no advertising, no analytics of your behaviour, no crash-reporting service and no attribution or “growth” SDK. The only third-party code in it is RevenueCat's subscription SDK, described above; everything else is part of iOS itself. Nothing tracks you across apps or websites, so iOS never shows you an App Tracking Transparency prompt for this app, and no advertising identifier is involved.

We do not use your information — because we do not have it — to train any AI model, and no photo, face or result is ever sent to a third-party AI service. All the intelligence in the app is Apple's, running on your device.

10Your choices and your rights

Because your information stays on your iPhone, you exercise most of your rights directly, without asking anyone:

  • See what is kept: section 4 lists everything that is kept, and the app shows you how many saved results are on your iPhone in Settings → Clear saved results.
  • Delete the saved results: Settings → Clear saved results.
  • Withdraw your agreement to face analysis: Settings → Withdraw face consent. The app stops analysing faces until you agree again, and every saved result is deleted.
  • Delete everything: remove the app from your iPhone. That erases the saved results, the settings and any temporary files from the device. A copy may still sit inside an iPhone backup you made earlier, until that backup is replaced or deleted — that backup is yours and Apple's, not ours.
  • Turn off a permission: iOS Settings, at any time, without losing anything else.

For the information we can actually reach — your subscription record at RevenueCat and any email you sent us — you may ask us to give you a copy, correct it, delete it, restrict or object to its use, or receive it in a portable form. Write to support@alkanfatih.com and we will answer within 30 days. We never charge for this and never ask you to create an account to do it.

11Region-specific information

Europe (GDPR) and the United Kingdom

The comparison happens on your iPhone, under your control, and we never receive or access any of it — using the app on your own family photos is your own personal activity, not something we process. To the extent the GDPR applies to that processing at all, our basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)), asked for before the first photo and withdrawable at any time in Settings; and, should any of it ever be treated as special-category data, that same explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)). For providing the app and the subscription we rely on performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b)); for answering support emails, on our legitimate interest in helping you (Art. 6(1)(f)).

The app does not process biometric data “for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person”: it neither identifies nor authenticates anyone. There is no profiling and no automated decision-making with legal or similarly significant effects — the percentages are entertainment.

Nothing you do with photos or faces is transferred anywhere, so for that there is no international transfer to consider. Two things do leave the EEA/UK: your subscription record reaches RevenueCat, Inc. in the United States, which acts as our processor under its data processing terms and the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses; and, if you write to us, your email reaches our mailbox in Türkiye, which has no adequacy decision — that is the only copy and we delete it when it is no longer needed. You have the right to lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority.

Illinois, Texas, Washington and other US biometric laws

The app is designed so that no face-derived information is ever in our possession or control: it is created and destroyed on your device, under your control, as set out in the schedule in section 6, which also serves as our public written retention and destruction policy. We do not sell, lease, trade, disclose or otherwise profit from any biometric identifier or biometric information, we never store a face template in any database, and we never use face data to identify anyone. The app asks for your written (electronic) consent before the first analysis, and lets you withdraw it and delete the stored results at any time.

California

We have never sold or shared personal information, and we never will — there is nothing to opt out of. Nothing from your photos, faces or results is collected at all. What we do receive is your subscription record through RevenueCat (an anonymous customer id and the purchase details, used to unlock Pro and to count subscriptions) and anything you put in an email to us. You may ask us to confirm, correct, delete or hand over a copy of either, and we will never discriminate against you for asking.

12Security

The app stores its files inside the iOS application sandbox, which no other app can read, and the two files it writes — the saved results and the temporary share copy — are marked with complete file protection, so iOS keeps them encrypted whenever your iPhone is locked. We run no servers and hold no database of our own: your photos, faces and results are never transmitted, so the strongest guarantee we can give is that we never have them in the first place. The subscription record lives with RevenueCat under its own security programme, and support emails with our mail provider. If we ever became aware of an incident affecting information we do hold, we would notify the people affected and the relevant authorities as the law requires.

14Changes to this policy

If the app changes, this policy changes with it, and the “Last updated” date above changes too. If we ever changed what the app does with faces, the app would explain the change and ask you to agree again before analysing anything. Beyond the subscription described in section 5, if the app were ever to send anything else off your device, we would ask for your permission first, clearly and separately — it would never happen quietly.

If Mom or Dad? is ever sold or handed to another developer, the only thing that could change hands is our support mailbox: there is no user database to transfer, because everything the app produces stays on your iPhone. We would update this page before any such change took effect.
