Who can use Wood Plans

Wood Plans is for adults. You must be 18 or older to use it.

Free preview

Making a plan and seeing its preview is free, with no set limit: the 3D model, the part and lumber counts, the first two rows of the cut list and the Plan checks warnings.

Photo reading uses an outside AI service and can sometimes be unavailable. You can always pick the type yourself or start from a template; the plan is made the same way.

Wood Plans Pro

Wood Plans Pro is an auto-renewing subscription with two plans, Weekly and Yearly. The app shows the price in your currency before you buy. There is no free trial.

Pro unlocks the full plan for every project: the complete cut list, the lumber shopping list, the cutting layout, the hardware list, the step-by-step assembly, PDF export and saving projects.

  • The subscription renews automatically unless you cancel it at least 24 hours before the current period ends.

Photos you send

Only send photos of furniture, and only photos you have the right to use. Avoid photos that show people or private details, and never send anything illegal or harmful. When you agree to photo reading, you allow Wood Plans to send the photo to Google Gemini so it can be read, as the Privacy Policy explains.

AI suggestions can be wrong

An AI service suggests the furniture type, a rough size, a likely material and some options, such as the number of shelves. It can be wrong. You confirm or change these on the Check the size screen. If you keep the suggested size, the plan uses that estimate, so measure the real piece or your space first. Wood Plans then calculates the parts with fixed rules and may round a size or change an option so the piece can be built. The Check the size screen shows the numbers the plan uses.

A plan is a guide

Real lumber varies: actual thickness and width, moisture, warp and your own saw kerf. Plans use standard US lumber and sheet sizes, even in mm; boards sold elsewhere may differ. Measure your boards and check every dimension before you cut.

Warnings in the app, such as shelf sag, tip-over or thin legs, are rules of thumb for typical wood, not engineering. They don't cover every risk, and Wood Plans does not say how much weight a piece can hold. Plans are for hobby use, not for children's furniture, for anything people climb on or hang from, or for parts of a building.

Safety

Woodworking tools can cause serious injury. Follow each tool's manual, use guards, eye and hearing protection and dust control, and never work beyond your experience.

  • Anchor tall pieces to a wall stud. Bookshelf plans 30 in (762 mm) or taller include an anti-tip kit for this.
  • Screw floating shelves into wall studs, as the plan shows. Drywall anchors alone will not hold them.
  • Outdoors, use cedar or an exterior finish, with coated or stainless screws.

You are responsible for how you build and use what you make.
