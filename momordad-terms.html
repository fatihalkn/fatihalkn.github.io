2What the app is — and isn't

Mom or Dad? takes three photos you choose — you, your mother and your father — lines the faces up at the eyes and measures how alike they look, feature by feature. It then prints a photo strip: an overall percentage, six feature verdicts (eyes, brows, nose, smile, face shape and skin tone), close-ups side by side for five of them, and a split portrait.

It is entertainment. The app compares how faces look in photographs. It is not a DNA, genetic, paternity, maternity or medical test, it does not analyse or infer any biological relationship, and its results are not evidence of anything. Never rely on a result for any decision about parentage, custody, inheritance, immigration, health or any other serious matter.

Results depend entirely on the photos: angle, lighting, expression, age, glasses, makeup, hair and photo quality all change them. Different photos of the same people can give different answers. “Skin tone” compares the colour of the cheeks as they appear in your photos — which lighting and the camera change — and is not a measure of ethnicity, race or ancestry. The same three photos give you the same result for as long as the app still remembers that strip — up to a year, and up to the 300 most recent strips, unless you clear the saved results.

The app needs a clearly visible, roughly front-facing face in each photo. Sometimes it will not find one, or will pick a different face in a group photo. That is a limitation of the app, not a statement about the photo.

5Face analysis and your consent

Before the first photo is added, the app explains what it does with faces and asks you to agree. The analysis uses Apple's on-device Vision framework: the app finds facial landmarks in each photo to line the faces up and compare them. It never identifies, recognises or authenticates anyone, it never creates or stores a face template, and nothing is ever uploaded. It does keep a short, non-reversible code of each photo on your device so that the same photos give the same result; the Privacy Policy describes exactly what that is. You can withdraw your agreement at any time in the app's Settings, which also deletes every saved result from your device. The Privacy Policy explains this in detail, including what is stored and for how long.

6Mom or Dad? Pro subscriptions

Free and Pro

Without a subscription you get the intro and one strip: the headline result, one feature ticket with its close-ups — the other five stay blurred behind a lock — and the split portrait and share card, both carrying the app's watermark. The free strip is counted on the iPhone you are using, not on your Apple Account. Re-running a strip the app still remembers does not use up that free strip.

Mom or Dad? Pro unlocks all six feature tickets — five with close-ups side by side, plus the skin-tone swatches — the split portraits in HD without the watermark (including saving them to your photo library), and unlimited new strips. Share cards keep a small “Mom or Dad? on the App Store” badge.

  • The free app itself can be shared with your family through Family Sharing, but the Pro subscription cannot: it applies to the Apple Account that bought it, on that account's devices.
